## Exploring the frontier of particle physics, from Dhahran to CERN

King Fahd University of Petroleum & Minerals (KFUPM) has recently joined the **[ATLAS Collaboration](https://atlas.cern)** at CERN's **[Large Hadron Collider(LHC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_Hadron_Collider)** as an Associate Member Institute. ATLAS is one of the largest scientific collaborations in the world: thousands of physicists and engineers from around 180 institutions working together to understand the fundamental building blocks of nature. ATLAS co-discovered the Higgs boson in 2012, leading to the 2013 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Our newly established group, led by Dr. Wasikul Islam, is based in the **[Department of Physics](https://physics.kfupm.edu.sa)** and the **[Interdisciplinary Research Center for Industrial Nuclear Energy (IRC-INE)](https://ri.kfupm.edu.sa/irc-ine)**. We work closely with colleagues at **[SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory (USA)](https://www6.slac.stanford.edu)** and across the ATLAS Collaboration. Our goal is simple and ambitious: to make KFUPM a home for frontier particle physics research in the region. That means doing world-class science, building detector technology on campus, and training the next generation of physicists in Saudi Arabia.

## Research directions

### Searching for new physics beyond the Standard Model
The Standard Model of particle physics is remarkably successful, but it leaves big questions unanswered: dark matter, the matter–antimatter asymmetry, and the origin of the Higgs mass scale. We search ATLAS collision data for signs of **new particles and phenomena beyond the Standard Model**. Our focus is on **model-independent searches and anomaly detection**: rather than looking only for what theorists predict, we let the data tell us where something unexpected may be hiding. We plan to develop and apply **unsupervised, physics-informed and fast-inference machine-learning methods**, as well as emerging **agentic-AI** tools for physics analysis.

### Detectors for the High-Luminosity LHC
From 2030 the LHC will enter its **High-Luminosity (HL-LHC)** era, delivering about ten times more data than before. To cope with this, ATLAS is building a brand-new all-silicon **Inner Tracker (ITk)**. Our group contributes to **ITk pixel detector** activities, including module assembly, testing, quality control and performance studies. We also work on the **tracking, vertexing and pile-up mitigation** algorithms needed to reconstruct up to 200 simultaneous proton collisions.

### Bridging theory and experiment
Good searches start with good questions. We work at the interface of **phenomenology and experiment**. This includes simulation and sensitivity studies for the HL-LHC and **future colliders**, and connecting new theoretical ideas to signatures that can actually be tested in the data.

## Training the next generation

Students and postdocs in our group become full participants in an international collaboration. They develop skills in:

- large-scale data analysis and statistics,
- machine learning and AI,
- scientific computing (Python / C++),
- silicon detectors and instrumentation,
- working in global scientific teams.

Members may also have opportunities to spend research time at **CERN** and **SLAC**.

We particularly welcome motivated students who are curious and willing to learn. Not every skill has to be in place on day one; we are committed to helping talented people grow into independent researchers.
